## What is reconstruction in this episode

All findings come from one study of 15 people with temporal lobe epilepsy, 10 of whom became seizure-free after surgery, and 15 people without epilepsy. In the 5 who still had seizures, nothing changed. The surgery itself, removing part of the temporal lobe, is not drawn. The arousal centres studied are small nuclei in the upper brainstem and the ventral tegmental area; they are drawn as the brainstem outline, and "frontal" neocortex is drawn on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, which are approximations. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- The "before" difference from controls is supported by the paper's own conclusion that "ARAS functional connectivity disturbances are present in TLE"; levels are illustrative.
- The vta-insula line is one of several possible ARAS-to-cortex lines; the paper reports ARAS structures together with fronto-parietal-insular neocortex.
- Read in full: González et al. 2020.

## The condition

### **brainstem-mid → insula** (connection) — Mixed evidence

Claim: Less in step than in people without epilepsy before surgery; more in step after surgery in people who became seizure-free, in one study. The arousal centres studied are small nuclei in the upper brainstem and the ventral tegmental area; they are drawn as the brainstem outline, and "frontal" neocortex is drawn on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, which are approximations.

On screen: peaks above baseline (0.45), ends at baseline (0).

- González HFJ, Goodale SE, Jacobs ML, Haas KF, Landman BA, Morgan VL, Englot DJ (2020). Brainstem Functional Connectivity Disturbances in Epilepsy may Recover After Successful Surgery. doi:10.1093/neuros/nyz128 — study, full-text
  > "ARAS functional connectivity disturbances are present in TLE but may recover after successful epilepsy surgery."
- González HFJ, Goodale SE, Jacobs ML, Haas KF, Landman BA, Morgan VL, Englot DJ (2020). Brainstem Functional Connectivity Disturbances in Epilepsy may Recover After Successful Surgery. doi:10.1093/neuros/nyz128 — study, full-text
  > "TLE patients who achieved long-term postoperative seizure freedom (10 of 15) demonstrated increases in functional connectivity between ARAS structures and fronto-parietal-insular neocortex compared to preoperative baseline"
- González HFJ, Goodale SE, Jacobs ML, Haas KF, Landman BA, Morgan VL, Englot DJ (2020). Brainstem Functional Connectivity Disturbances in Epilepsy may Recover After Successful Surgery. doi:10.1093/neuros/nyz128 — study, full-text
  > "with postoperative connectivity patterns resembling controls’ connectivity."
