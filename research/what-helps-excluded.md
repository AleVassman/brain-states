# What helps: treatments looked at and not included

The rule (from `docs/2026-11-18-what-helps-design.md` §5): a treatment gets an episode only if at least one study, or a pooled analysis, scanned humans before and after that treatment and reports a regional change with a direction, on a region the brain model has.

| Treatment | Condition | Why it is not included |
|---|---|---|
| Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) | Depression | Only one before-and-after study was read (Taylor et al. 2018, sham-controlled, doi:10.1016/j.jad.2018.02.019). In it, active TMS did not do better than sham on symptoms, and connectivity changes went with improvement in both groups, so no TMS-specific change could be drawn. The authors note their stimulation target was non-standard. Open-label before-and-after TMS imaging studies exist and were not read; TMS could be added after reading one. |
| CBT for PTSD (Felmingham et al. 2007) | PTSD | Relevant, but no abstract or full text could be retrieved. PTSD is covered by the exposure therapy episode. |
| Recovery after concussion | Concussion | The studies found (for example Meier et al. 2015) follow natural recovery over time, not a treatment; the concussion episode already has a recovery phase from the same paper. |
| Disease-modifying drugs for multiple sclerosis | Multiple sclerosis | Pooled analyses found (for example Tsivgoulis et al. 2015, Branger et al. 2016) report slower loss of whole-brain volume, not a change in a particular region, so there is no region to draw. Not read in full. |
| Anti-seizure medication | Epileptic seizure | In the starting list but not searched in depth. Epilepsy surgery is included. |
| Treatments for low blood sugar, Huntington's disease, infection | Low blood sugar, Huntington's, infection | Not in the starting list of candidates (§5 of the design) and not searched. |
