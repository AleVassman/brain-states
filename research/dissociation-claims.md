## What is reconstruction in this episode

This account comes mainly from small studies of people with post-traumatic stress disorder listening to accounts of their own trauma, summarised in one review. It is a proposal: more activity in regulating regions alongside less in emotional ones is consistent with suppression, but that does not prove one causes the other. A later systematic review concluded that dissociation cannot be pinned to a few regions and differs with the kind of symptoms, and a pilot study in borderline personality disorder found the insula more active with dissociation, not less. How an episode ends has not been studied; the last phase is an assumption. Dissociation in borderline personality disorder may not match the pattern found in trauma studies.

## Notes from the research pass

- No line is drawn between the medial prefrontal cortex and the amygdala. The finding is that one is more active as the other is less, which a bright line would show as the two working together; it is described in the amygdala text instead.
- No typical-response comparison. The informative contrast in the literature is between two responses to a trauma reminder, dissociating and being flooded, not between a condition and a typical brain. The flooded pattern is described in the amygdala text and not drawn.
- Reduced pain sensitivity during dissociation is from Ludäscher et al. 2010 ("Dissociative psychopathology scores were significantly higher and pain sensitivity was lower after the dissociation-inducing script was read compared with the neutral script."), a pilot study in borderline personality disorder without a comparison group. It is mentioned in the right insula text, with its quote, and not drawn.
- Not drawn: the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, superior frontal regions, posterior association areas and basal ganglia named by Roydeva & Reinders 2021. The abstract lists them as markers without saying whether they are more or less active, so no direction could be shown.
- The review of dissociation in borderline personality disorder specifically (Krause-Utz et al. 2017) could not be downloaded, so the episode does not claim the trauma-study pattern holds in borderline personality disorder.
- Read as abstracts only: Roydeva & Reinders 2021, Lotfinia et al. 2020.

## Order of events

1. **Too much** (seconds) — start of the episode.
2. **Regulation rises** (seconds) — order **reconstructed**: The studies compare whole periods of listening to a trauma account with a neutral one. That the regulating regions rise first and the emotional regions fall second is the logic of the suppression account, not something timed.
3. **Detached** (seconds to minutes) — order **reconstructed**: The quieter emotional regions are measured over the same period as the more active regulating regions, as correlations. Showing them as a later step follows the suppression account.
4. **Easing** (minutes or longer (not studied)) — order **reconstructed**: No source read for this episode followed the end of a dissociative state. The return towards baseline is assumed.

A systematic review across diagnoses also lists the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex among the regions that mark dissociation. Another review found that dissociation cannot be tied to a few regions.

### **amygdala** — Still debated

Claim: In one study of people with post-traumatic stress after road accidents, the more active the dissociation-related part of the medial prefrontal cortex was, the less active the amygdala. This is read as the regulating regions suppressing the emotional ones, which is an interpretation. The opposite pattern, too little regulation, is found in people who are flooded by the memory instead. A single-study finding with a proposed explanation.

On screen: peaks below baseline (-0.5), ends at baseline (0).

### **insula-r** — Still debated

Claim: The front of the right insula was less active the stronger the dissociative response to a trauma reminder. The evidence conflicts: a pilot study in borderline personality disorder with post-traumatic stress found the insula more active with higher dissociation scores, and that study had no comparison group. In that same small study, people felt pain less after listening to a script that brought on dissociation.
