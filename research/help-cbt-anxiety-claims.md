## What is reconstruction in this episode

The pooled findings come from an analysis of 22 studies of CBT for depression and anxiety disorders; the anxiety studies cover several different disorders. The two other parts come from single studies of social anxiety disorder, one of 14 people with no untreated comparison group (so the changes cannot be credited to CBT alone) and one randomised trial of 75 people of whom about 60 had usable scans, using different tasks, which is why the medial prefrontal cortex goes down in one and up in the other. The pooled analysis names the superior frontal gyrus, which is not drawn as its own area. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- Goldin et al. 2013 report "greater dorsomedial prefrontal cortex-amygdala inverse functional connectivity"; an inverse relationship is not drawn as a line, following the project rule.
- The medial prefrontal cortex is "debated" because the two single studies find opposite directions with different tasks; both are shown in their own phase.
- Ren et al. 2025 report the superior frontal gyrus (increased at rest) and the left dorsal entorhinal cortex (decreased during tasks); neither is a separate region in the brain model.
- The "rethinking" findings compare CBT with a waiting list, not before with after; the phase says so.
- Read in full: Klumpp et al. 2013, Goldin et al. 2013. Read as abstract only: Ren et al. 2025.

## The condition

### **mpfc-r** — Still debated

Claim: The right dorsomedial prefrontal cortex was more active than in people without social anxiety while viewing angry faces before treatment, and less active after CBT, in one study of 14 people. In a randomised trial (about 60 people scanned), the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex was more active after CBT while reacting to and rethinking negative beliefs about themselves. The direction depends on the task.

On screen: peaks above baseline (0.4), ends at baseline (0).

- Klumpp H, Fitzgerald DA, Phan KL (2013). Neural predictors and mechanisms of cognitive behavioral therapy on threat processing in social anxiety disorder. doi:10.1016/j.pnpbp.2013.05.004 — study, full-text
  > "The interaction was driven by relatively greater activity during threat processing in SAD, which significantly reduced after CBT but did not significantly predict response to CBT."
- Klumpp H, Fitzgerald DA, Phan KL (2013). Neural predictors and mechanisms of cognitive behavioral therapy on threat processing in social anxiety disorder. doi:10.1016/j.pnpbp.2013.05.004 — study, full-text
  > "Importantly, there was a significant Group x Time interaction for right insula and prefrontal regions, specifically right medial orbitofrontal and right dorsomedial PFC"
- Klumpp H, Fitzgerald DA, Phan KL (2013). Neural predictors and mechanisms of cognitive behavioral therapy on threat processing in social anxiety disorder. doi:10.1016/j.pnpbp.2013.05.004 — study, full-text
  > "Moreover, a group by time interaction was revealed in prefrontal regions (dorsomedial, medial gyrus) and insula."
- Goldin PR, Ziv M, Jazaieri H, Hahn K, Heimberg R, Gross JJ (2013). Impact of cognitive behavioral therapy for social anxiety disorder on the neural dynamics of cognitive reappraisal of negative self-beliefs: randomized clinical trial. doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2013.234 — study, full-text
  > "During reactivity trials, compared to waitlist, Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy produced (a) greater reduction in negative emotion ratings and (b) greater blood oxygen-level dependent signal magnitude in medial prefrontal cortex."
- Goldin PR, Ziv M, Jazaieri H, Hahn K, Heimberg R, Gross JJ (2013). Impact of cognitive behavioral therapy for social anxiety disorder on the neural dynamics of cognitive reappraisal of negative self-beliefs: randomized clinical trial. doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2013.234 — study, full-text
  > "During cognitive reappraisal trials, compared to waitlist, Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy produced (c) greater reduction in negative emotion ratings, (d) greater blood oxygen-level dependent signal magnitude in dorsolateral and dorsomedial prefrontal cortex"
- Goldin PR, Ziv M, Jazaieri H, Hahn K, Heimberg R, Gross JJ (2013). Impact of cognitive behavioral therapy for social anxiety disorder on the neural dynamics of cognitive reappraisal of negative self-beliefs: randomized clinical trial. doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2013.234 — study, full-text
