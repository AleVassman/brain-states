## What is reconstruction in this episode

The findings come from two pooled analyses of studies in which people were shown cues related to the substance they use, compared with neutral cues. They average over the viewing period, so the order of events is reconstructed. Which regions respond depends partly on whether people were seeking treatment. A model of addiction places frontal control regions in disrupted inhibitory control, but the sources read give no direction for them during a craving, so they are described rather than drawn. People's experiences differ.

## Notes from the research pass

- The insula is not drawn. Chase et al. 2011 found cue-related activation "but not insula cortex"; the Koob & Volkow model includes the insula in craving, so the evidence is mixed.
- The left middle frontal gyrus rose with craving in Chase et al. 2011, but it was a correlation with craving strength, not an average response, so it is not drawn.
- Frontal control regions are described, not drawn: Koob & Volkow 2010 place "the cingulate gyrus, dorsolateral prefrontal, and inferior frontal cortices in disrupted inhibitory control" but give no direction for a craving episode.
- The right pallidum (Kühn & Gallinat 2011, alcohol) is not in the brain model.
- No typical-response comparison is drawn: the studies compare reminders with neutral pictures in people who use the substance.
- Read in full: Chase et al. 2011. Read as abstracts only: Kühn & Gallinat 2011, Koob & Volkow 2010.

## The condition

### **nucleus-accumbens** — Well established

Claim: The ventral striatum responds to reminders of the substance, across nicotine, alcohol and cocaine, in two pooled analyses, and its response also rises with how strong the craving feels. Drawn on the nucleus accumbens, the main part of the ventral striatum.

On screen: peaks above baseline (0.75), ends at baseline (0).

- Chase HW, Eickhoff SB, Laird AR, Hogarth L (2011). The neural basis of drug stimulus processing and craving: an activation likelihood estimation meta-analysis. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2011.05.025 — meta-analysis, full-text
  > "Cue-related activation was observed in the ventral striatum (across both subgroups), amygdala (in the treatment-seeking subgroup and overall), and orbitofrontal cortex (in the nontreatment-seeking subgroup and overall) but not insula cortex."
- Kühn S, Gallinat J (2011). Common biology of craving across legal and illegal drugs - a quantitative meta-analysis of cue-reactivity brain response. doi:10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07590.x — meta-analysis, abstract-only
  > "We found direct overlap between nicotine, alcohol and cocaine cue reactivity in the ventral striatum."
- Kühn S, Gallinat J (2011). Common biology of craving across legal and illegal drugs - a quantitative meta-analysis of cue-reactivity brain response. doi:10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07590.x — meta-analysis, abstract-only
  > "The ACC, right pallidum and ventral striatum were related to drug cue reactivity as well as self-reported craving, suggesting that this set of brain regions constitutes the core circuit of drug craving in nicotine and alcohol addiction."
