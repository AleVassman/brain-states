## What is reconstruction in this episode

All findings come from one study: people with depression who were not taking other medication received one infusion, and were scanned a day later. The study measures how much each area moves in step with the rest of the brain at rest, which is not the same as activity. The source names the prefrontal cortex without saying which part for the main finding; it is drawn on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, which is an assumption. How long these changes last was not measured here. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- Brightness is global brain connectivity, not activity; said in the description and on each region.
- Caudate and insula increases were reported in responders only, not the whole group; the explanations say so.
- Abdallah et al. 2017 report that 2174 voxels differed from controls before and 310 after; summarised in the last caption.
- Read in full: Abdallah et al. 2017.

## The condition

### **dlpfc** — Mixed evidence

Claim: Less in step with the rest of the brain than in people without depression before the infusion, and more in step a day after it, in one study. The source names the prefrontal cortex; it is drawn on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, which is an assumption. Responders showed the increase in the lateral prefrontal cortex. Brightness stands for global connectivity, not activity.

On screen: peaks above baseline (0.45), ends at baseline (0).

- Abdallah CG, Averill LA, Collins KA, Geha P, Schwartz J, Averill C, DeWilde KE, et al. (2017). Ketamine Treatment and Global Brain Connectivity in Major Depression. doi:10.1038/npp.2016.186 — study, full-text
  > "MDD group showed reduced GBCr in the prefrontal cortex (PFC) but increased GBCr in the posterior cingulate, precuneus, lingual gyrus, and cerebellum."
- Abdallah CG, Averill LA, Collins KA, Geha P, Schwartz J, Averill C, DeWilde KE, et al. (2017). Ketamine Treatment and Global Brain Connectivity in Major Depression. doi:10.1038/npp.2016.186 — study, full-text
  > "Ketamine significantly increased GBCr in the PFC and reduced GBCr in the cerebellum."
- Abdallah CG, Averill LA, Collins KA, Geha P, Schwartz J, Averill C, DeWilde KE, et al. (2017). Ketamine Treatment and Global Brain Connectivity in Major Depression. doi:10.1038/npp.2016.186 — study, full-text
  > "Responders to ketamine showed increased GBCr in the lateral PFC, caudate, and insula."
