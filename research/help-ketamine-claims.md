## What is reconstruction in this episode

All findings come from one study of 18 people with depression that had not improved with two earlier antidepressants, and 25 people without depression. There was no placebo infusion. People had stopped antidepressants for only a week, so earlier medication may still have had effects. The study measures how much each area moves in step with the rest of the brain at rest, which is not the same as activity. "Lateral prefrontal cortex" is drawn on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, an approximation. Differences between responders and non-responders were small, preliminary comparisons. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- Brightness is global brain connectivity, not activity.
- The within-person change is right lateral PFC up and left cerebellum down (paired test); the left DLPFC is drawn only before.
- The insula is a responder-versus-non-responder difference, said in its explanation; the caudate finding (post-treatment responders vs non-responders) is not drawn.
- Read in full: Abdallah et al. 2017.

### **dlpfc-r** — Mixed evidence

Claim: The prefrontal cortex was less in step with the rest of the brain than in people without depression before the infusion; a day after it, the right lateral prefrontal cortex was more in step, in one study. Drawn on the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, an approximation. Brightness stands for global connectivity, not activity.
