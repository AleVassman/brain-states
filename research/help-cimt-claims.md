## What is reconstruction in this episode

The findings come from one randomised trial: 16 people received constraint-induced therapy and 20 a comparison therapy, which showed no grey matter increases. The people had had their stroke months or years earlier. The grey matter increases covered the hand and arm regions of the motor and sensory cortex, the front of the supplementary motor area and part of the premotor area; the whole motor and sensory strips are drawn, which is larger than the areas that changed. A pooled analysis of 22 studies of recovery after stroke found that activity during movement shifts back towards the damaged side as people recover; that is a different measure, described but not drawn, because the damaged side differs between people. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- Tang et al. 2015 (meta-analysis, 22 trials) describe activity shifting towards the damaged hemisphere in sensorimotor and premotor cortex with good recovery; not drawn because the side of damage varies, mentioned in the caveat.
- Read in full: Gauthier et al. 2008. Read as abstract only: Tang et al. 2015.

### **motor-cortex** — Mixed evidence

Claim: Grey matter increased in motor areas on both sides after constraint-induced therapy, in a randomised trial; the comparison therapy showed no increase. Brightness stands for volume, not activity.

On screen: peaks above baseline (0.5), ends at baseline (0).

- Gauthier LV, Taub E, Perkins C, Ortmann M, Mark VW, Uswatte G (2008). Remodeling the brain: plastic structural brain changes produced by different motor therapies after stroke. doi:10.1161/strokeaha.107.502229 — study, full-text
  > "There were profuse increases in gray matter in sensory and motor areas both contralateral and ipsilateral to the affected arm that were bilaterally symmetrical, as well as bilaterally in the hippocampus."
- Gauthier LV, Taub E, Perkins C, Ortmann M, Mark VW, Uswatte G (2008). Remodeling the brain: plastic structural brain changes produced by different motor therapies after stroke. doi:10.1161/strokeaha.107.502229 — study, full-text
  > "The large clusters of voxels showing gray matter increases in sensory and motor areas were bilaterally symmetrical and, when cross-referenced with standard brain atlases, encompassed the hand/arm regions of primary sensory and motor cortices as well as the anterior supplementary motor area and portions of the premotor area."
- Gauthier LV, Taub E, Perkins C, Ortmann M, Mark VW, Uswatte G (2008). Remodeling the brain: plastic structural brain changes produced by different motor therapies after stroke. doi:10.1161/strokeaha.107.502229 — study, full-text
