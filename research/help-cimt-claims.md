## What is reconstruction in this episode

The findings come from one randomised trial: 16 people received the full therapy and 20 the same therapy without the transfer package; the comparison group also improved, but showed no grey matter increases. The difference between groups was clear on one side and in the hippocampus, and only marginal on the other side. Eleven people with large strokes in the movement areas were left out of the movement-area analysis. The increases covered the hand and arm regions, the front of the supplementary motor area and part of the premotor area; the whole motor and sensory strips are drawn, which is larger than the areas that changed. Whether the changes last was not known. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- The comparison group received "all the components of CI therapy except for the transfer package"; the description and caveat say so.
- Tang et al. 2015 (meta-analysis, 22 trials) describe activity shifting towards the damaged hemisphere with good recovery; not drawn because the side of damage varies.
- Read in full: Gauthier et al. 2008.

Claim: Grey matter increased in the hand and arm area of the motor cortex on both sides after the full therapy, in a randomised trial. Compared with the group without the transfer package, the difference was significant on one side and marginal on the other. Brightness stands for grey matter, not activity.
