## What is reconstruction in this episode

The volume increase comes from a combined analysis of 281 patients at 10 sites and from a study of 43 patients. The increase is small, a few percent, and grows with the number of sessions. The larger analysis found that a bigger increase did not go with a better outcome, so the growth does not explain why ECT works. Only the hippocampus and amygdala were measured in these studies. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- In this episode brightness shows volume, not activity; said in the description and on each region.
- The two studies disagree on whether the volume change relates to outcome: Joshi et al. 2016 found a relation with improvement; the larger Oltedal et al. 2018 analysis did not find a positive one. Both are cited and the caption follows the larger analysis.
- Read in full: Joshi et al. 2016, Oltedal et al. 2018.

## The condition

### **hippocampus** — Well established

Claim: Smaller than in people without depression before treatment, in one study. Larger after a course of ECT, in that study and in a combined analysis of 281 patients, by about 0.28% per session. The larger increase did not go with a better outcome. Brightness stands for volume, not activity.
