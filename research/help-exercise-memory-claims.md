## What is reconstruction in this episode

These are studies of mild cognitive impairment, not of Alzheimer's disease itself, and they do not show that exercise prevents or slows Alzheimer's. The first trial included 86 women aged 70 to 80, of whom only 29 had brain scans both before and after; in the exercise group the hippocampus did not grow, it was maintained, and a larger left hippocampus went, unexpectedly, with worse performance on one memory measure. The second study had blood-flow data for 15 people with mild cognitive impairment and 17 without, and no group that did not exercise. The left anterior cingulate is drawn on the pregenual cingulate and the right inferior frontal gyrus on the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, both approximations. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## Notes from the research pass

- Two separate trials are shown in separate phases, each with its own comparison; they are not stitched into one before and after.
- Hippocampus: ten Brinke et al. 2015 call it "maintenance rather than increase"; drawn as the difference from the comparison group, and the text says so.
- Insula: the difference from people without memory problems disappeared; no within-group fall is reported, so none is drawn.
- Read in full: ten Brinke et al. 2015, Alfini et al. 2019.
