## What is reconstruction in this episode

These studies use a mild immune challenge in healthy volunteers and scan them about two hours later. That is a stand-in for a real infection. In the vaccine study body temperature did not rise, while in the endotoxin study it did. Fever is part of the same brain-directed response; in rodents it is set off in a small area of the hypothalamus, the median preoptic nucleus, which is too small to draw here, so nothing is drawn for fever. Most findings come from two small groups of volunteers, sixteen men in one and thirty-nine people in the other, and several were seen only while people did a particular task in the scanner. A pooled analysis confirms that inflammation goes with changes in these regions, without regard to their direction. How the brain recovers as the infection clears was not studied.

## Notes from the research pass

- The substantia nigra is not drawn. Brydon et al. 2008 report its reactivity as "markedly perturbed" and its activity as "associated with" slower reactions, without a direction that could be shown. It is mentioned in a caption.
- The state is listed as "Infection: feeling ill", not "Infection and fever", because fever is not shown.
- Fever is not drawn. Saper et al. 2012 (full text) place the fever trigger in rodents in the median preoptic nucleus, a small part of the hypothalamus that is not in the brain model. Eisenberger et al. 2010 report that endotoxin raised body temperature; Harrison et al. 2009 report that the vaccine did not.
- The first phase draws no region. Dantzer et al. 2008 (abstract): "innate immune cells produce pro-inflammatory cytokines that act on the brain to cause sickness behaviour", without saying where.
- The hypothalamus, amygdala, hippocampus and brainstem are listed by the pooled analysis (Kraynak et al. 2018) as consistently affected by inflammation, but the abstract gives no direction, so they are not drawn.
- No typical-response comparison: the volunteers were healthy, so this is the ordinary response. Each study compared the immune challenge with a placebo in the same or similar people.
- The two Harrison 2009 papers and Brydon 2008 each describe sixteen healthy men given typhoid vaccination and appear to report the same volunteers, so they are not three independent confirmations.
- Read in full: both Harrison 2009 papers, Eisenberger 2010, Kraynak 2018, Saper 2012. Read as abstracts only: Brydon 2008, Dantzer 2008.

## Order of events

1. **The alarm is raised** (the first hours) — start of the episode.
2. **Tired and slowed** (two to three hours in the studies) — order **reconstructed**: Volunteers were scanned once, two hours after the injection, and their mood and tiredness were asked about at two and three hours. The split into separate steps is a way of presenting those findings, not a measured sequence.
3. **Low mood, less pleasure** (two to three hours in the studies) — order **reconstructed**: The mood drop was significant at three hours, an hour after the scan. Placing it after the tiredness is a way of presenting the findings; the studies did not time the two against each other.
4. **Getting better** (days (not studied)) — order **reconstructed**: No source read followed volunteers through recovery. The return to rest is assumed.

## The condition

### **insula** — Mixed evidence

Claim: Regions that represent the internal state of the body became more active under inflammation while volunteers did a demanding task, and their activity predicted how tired and foggy each person felt. They included the mid and front parts of the insula on both sides. A pooled analysis lists the insula among regions consistently affected by inflammation.

  > "Performance of the Stroop task under inflammation activated brain regions encoding representations of internal bodily state."
- Harrison NA, Brydon L, Walker C, Gray MA, Steptoe A, Dolan RJ, Critchley HD (2009). Neural origins of human sickness in interoceptive responses to inflammation. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.007 — study, full-text
  > "activity within interoceptive brain regions also predicted individual differences in inflammation-associated but not placebo-associated fatigue and confusion"
- Harrison NA, Brydon L, Walker C, Gray MA, Steptoe A, Dolan RJ, Critchley HD (2009). Neural origins of human sickness in interoceptive responses to inflammation. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.007 — study, full-text

### **sgacc** — Mixed evidence

Claim: The more a volunteer's mood fell after vaccination, the more active this region was while they looked at emotional faces. The region is one implicated in depression, and the authors take this to suggest a shared basis for low mood in illness and in depression. From one study of sixteen men. It is drawn at rest: the study found this only as a link with how far each person's mood fell, not as an average rise across everyone.

On screen: peaks at baseline (0), ends at baseline (0).

- Harrison NA, Brydon L, Walker C, Gray MA, Steptoe A, Critchley HD (2009). Inflammation causes mood changes through alterations in subgenual cingulate activity and mesolimbic connectivity. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.015 — study, full-text
  > "Inflammation-associated mood deterioration correlated with enhanced activity within subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sACC) (a region implicated in the etiology of depression) during emotional face processing."
- Harrison NA, Brydon L, Walker C, Gray MA, Steptoe A, Critchley HD (2009). Inflammation causes mood changes through alterations in subgenual cingulate activity and mesolimbic connectivity. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.015 — study, full-text
  > "The sACC activity reflected the interaction between processing of emotional facial expressions and inflammation-associated total mood change rather than as a main effect of processing emotional faces."

### **nucleus-accumbens** — Mixed evidence

Claim: Drawn on the nucleus accumbens, the main part of the ventral striatum. The finding is a weaker response to the prospect of a reward, not lower activity at rest. The ventral striatum responded less to signs of a coming reward in people given a mild immune challenge than in people given a placebo, and that weaker response accounted for part of their drop in mood. From one study of thirty-nine people. A pooled analysis also lists the striatum, mainly the caudate and putamen, among regions affected by inflammation, without regard to direction.

On screen: peaks below baseline (-0.55), ends at baseline (0).

- Eisenberger NI, Berkman ET, Inagaki TK, Rameson LT, Mashal NM, Irwin MR (2010). Inflammation-induced anhedonia: endotoxin reduces ventral striatum responses to reward. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2010.06.010 — study, full-text
