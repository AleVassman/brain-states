## What is reconstruction in this episode

All findings come from one trial of 42 people with panic disorder and agoraphobia who were not taking medication, compared with 42 people without the condition scanned at the same times. The task was a fear-learning task, not a panic attack. The study names the anterior cingulate without saying which part; it is drawn on the dorsal part, which is an assumption. The source reports no pre-treatment difference, so the first part is drawn at rest. These are averages across groups of people, not a picture of any one person's brain. Treatments help many people, not everyone, and in different ways. This is not medical advice: talk to a doctor or therapist about what is right for you.

## The condition

### **vlpfc-l** — Mixed evidence

Claim: The left inferior frontal gyrus responded less to a learned warning signal after CBT, compared with people without panic disorder scanned at the same times, in one trial of 42 patients. The drop went with fewer agoraphobia symptoms.

On screen: peaks below baseline (-0.45), ends at baseline (0).

- Kircher T, Arolt V, Jansen A, Pyka M, Reinhardt I, Kellermann T, Konrad C, et al. (2013). Effect of cognitive-behavioral therapy on neural correlates of fear conditioning in panic disorder. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.07.026 — study, abstract-only
  > "After CBT, patients compared to control subjects revealed reduced activation for the conditioned response (CS+ > CS-) in the left inferior frontal gyrus (IFG)."
- Kircher T, Arolt V, Jansen A, Pyka M, Reinhardt I, Kellermann T, Konrad C, et al. (2013). Effect of cognitive-behavioral therapy on neural correlates of fear conditioning in panic disorder. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.07.026 — study, abstract-only
  > "This activation reduction was correlated with reduction in agoraphobic symptoms from t1 to t2."
- Kircher T, Arolt V, Jansen A, Pyka M, Reinhardt I, Kellermann T, Konrad C, et al. (2013). Effect of cognitive-behavioral therapy on neural correlates of fear conditioning in panic disorder. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.07.026 — study, abstract-only
